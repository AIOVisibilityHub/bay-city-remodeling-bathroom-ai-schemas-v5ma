Carefully detach all cabinet doors, drawer fronts, and hardware pulls from the frames. Number each door and matching opening with labeled painter's tape so every piece returns to its exact original location during reassembly, preventing alignment headaches later.

Wash the cabinet boxes, doors, and drawer faces thoroughly using a heavy-duty degreasing cleaner to eliminate cooking oils, grease, and fingerprints. Rinse the surfaces with clean water and let them dry completely, as leftover residue prevents paint from bonding properly.

Fill gouges, dents, and unwanted hardware holes with durable wood filler, allowing it to dry before sanding smooth. Lightly scuff-sand all exterior surfaces with medium-to-fine grit sandpaper to break the factory sheen, then vacuum up all dust and wipe down with a tack cloth.

Cover countertops, appliances, floors, and open cabinet interiors with plastic sheeting and professional masking tape. Ensure proper room ventilation to manage fumes and control airborne dust while working inside the home.

Apply an even coat of stain-blocking, bonding primer to all prepared cabinet boxes, doors, and drawer fronts using a fine spray rig or high-density foam roller. Allow the primer to dry fully according to product instructions, then gently sand flat to remove any raised grain.

Apply two thin, uniform coats of premium cabinet enamel, allowing appropriate dry time between applications. Avoid rushing this stage; consistent coverage and proper drying build a tough, washable surface that holds up to daily kitchen demands.

Let the finish cure past initial dryness so the coating develops true working hardness before reattaching hinges and hardware. Painting kitchen cabinets might sound like a simple weekend DIY project, but it is one of the easiest tasks to get wrong if you cut corners. Here are the most frequent mistakes we see homeowners encounter:

  • Skipping the degreasing step: Cooking oils cling to cabinet surfaces, and painting over them causes flaking, bubbling, and poor paint adhesion.
  • Forgetting to label doors and hardware: Cabinet hinges and doors settle into specific alignments; mixing them up makes reinstallation a frustrating puzzle.
  • Bypassing bonding primer: Standard wall primer will not bond well to slick factory clears, resulting in chipped edges within weeks.
  • Sanding through delicate veneers: Being too aggressive with coarse sandpaper can ruin the thin wood veneer layer on cabinet door panels.
  • Confusing dry time with cure time: Just because paint feels dry to the touch does not mean it has hardened. Putting doors back in service too early causes dents and sticking.
  • Over-applying heavy coats: Thick paint leads to noticeable drips, runs, and a soft finish that scratches easily.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

The secret to durable cabinets is thorough surface preparation, correct product selection, and patience during curing. In older properties, especially homes built prior to 1978, paint disturbance may also require lead-safe protocols. Deciding between a dedicated walk-in pantry and a custom tall cabinet pull-out unit comes down to available square footage, kitchen layout, and your cooking habits. Both approaches improve storage, but they solve distinct problems.

How to Compare Your Options

  • Space requirements: Walk-in pantries require sufficient floor area not only for perimeter shelving but also for comfortable aisle walkways and door clearances. Cabinet pull-outs fit right into existing wall cabinet runs without demanding extra floor depth.
  • Visibility and organization: Walk-ins let you scan all items at a glance across open shelves, while pull-out pantries bring deep baskets and racks directly to eye level, preventing hidden clutter.
  • Project complexity: A tall cabinet pull-out installation can often fit into an existing cabinetry line with minimal structural impact. In contrast, building a walk-in frequently involves framing new drywall, rerouting existing wiring, adjusting trim, and updating lighting.
  • Budget and timeline: Cabinet units are faster to install once built, while framed walk-in rooms take longer because they involve multi-trade coordination, including drywall finishing and painting.

If your layout requires removing partitions, moving switches, or customizing structural framing to fit your chosen layout, professional planning ensures clean execution.
